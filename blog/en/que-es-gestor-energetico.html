What is an energy manager?

3 MIN READ · Published: August 29, 2026
GE

Engineering Department

Gestiones Eléctricas SAS

Executive Summary: What does an energy manager do in a company? This specialist analyzes consumption profiles, negotiates utility contracts, conducts power quality audits, and implements solar self-generation or cogeneration solutions. Their objective is to maximize efficiency and significantly reduce monthly billing costs without compromising production.

The energy manager is a company or professional capable of finding the best energy solution for a community of owners, an office building, a factory or any other type of property. The manager will be in charge of studying its typology and looking for all those aspects that can be improved with a single objective: energy saving. Among the functions of the energy manager are to continuously monitor energy consumption, control the correct operation of the installation and look for alternatives for energy savings through the application of other fuels or the implementation of new technologies, etc. In this way, an energy management company is able to provide the best service and comfort to neighbors, but ensuring savings. In fact, on average, an installation with energy management consumes 15% less energy than an installation that only has traditional maintenance. You may be wondering how this is achieved, very simple: the energy management company puts the latest technologies at your service to ensure maximum efficiency in the operation of the installation and this allows you to achieve energy savings.  However, what is really important is that, in the event that any unforeseen event arises that causes the facility to need more fuel, it is the energy services company that takes care of it and without economically affecting the neighbors. For this reason, it is said that energy services companies assume an economic risk, since in the event that the facilities did not function optimally, it would be the energy services company that would bear the costs of this inefficiency. This position is on the rise in recent times, various young professionals in the sector, who have just made the leap into the world of work, opt for training in this field that appears vital for the future of the industrial and energy world. Many other professionals, with years of experience, are also beginning to train in energy management, thus renewing their knowledge and beginning this learning to be able to get more out of the profession they currently occupy, or simply to opt for other job opportunities.
